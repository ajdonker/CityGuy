| Field | Details |

| Age | 30 |

| Location | Beijing and Cesena |

## Why Was He Chosen? Why Is He Appropriate?

The participant is a British citizen who has lived in Beijing for eight years. Despite his long-term residence, he has limited proficiency in spoken and written Chinese. Furthermore, restrictions on Google services create additional difficulties when navigating commonly used Chinese applications. Given these challenges, I would classify him as an extreme user in our UX research.

## How Was He Recruited?

We were friends a few years ago, and we had many misunderstandings because neither of us spoke the other's language very well.

## Where Was the Interview Conducted?

The interview was conducted via video call.

## What Did You Observe and Ask?

- **Language barriers:** His limited Chinese makes some tools and specific local information difficult to access.
- **Social comfort matters:** He wants welcoming places where he and his friends can feel comfortable and at home.
- **Missing experiential information:** Photos and reviews do not always reveal crowding, noise, or how welcoming a venue will feel.
- **Community supports trust:** He relies heavily on friends and WeChat groups with shared experiences. He retains that trust even when their recommendations produce different experiences.
- **Group familiarity affects sharing:** He hesitates to contribute when new to a group and becomes more willing after making friends.
- **Reporting happens indirectly:** He has reported problems to venue owners, but not directly to public authorities.

## Needs and Possible Solutions

### 1. Accessing Information Across Language Barriers

He needs to understand and access local information despite language barriers.

**Possible solutions:**

- Multilingual place descriptions.
- Translated community reviews.
- Natural-language search in his preferred language.
- Bilingual questions and answers.
- Visual summaries of facilities and activities.

### 2. Finding Welcoming Places to Socialize

He needs to find places where he feels welcome and comfortable socializing.

**Possible solutions:**

- Reviews describing visitors’ experiences of feeling welcome.
- Community-organized visits to unfamiliar venues.

### 3. Assessing Recommendations for Credibility and Relevance

He needs to assess whether recommendations are credible and relevant to his preferences.

**Possible solutions:**

- Recommendations from familiar community members.
- Reviews that explain the reviewer’s preferences.
- Timestamps and recent visitor photos.
- Separate fields for factual details and subjective impressions.

### 4. Feeling Comfortable Before Contributing

He needs to feel socially comfortable before contributing information.

**Possible solutions:**

- Smaller interest-based groups.
- Introductions for newcomers.
- Local meetups that help members connect.
- Opportunities to reply to existing discussions before starting posts.
- Control over whether contributions reach friends, a group, or the public.

## Additional Note

Reggie cares about what a place offers and how he and his friends will be treated as foreign residents.
