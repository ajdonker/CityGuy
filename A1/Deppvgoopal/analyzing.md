## Why Was He Chosen? Why Is He Appropriate?

He was our final interviewee. All our previous interviewees were in their 20s or 30s, so we wanted to recruit an older participant. He was in his 40s and owned a business, whereas all the other participants were students or employees. Therefore, I selected him as our final participant.

## How Was He Recruited?

I posted a message on a Chinese social media platform called REDnote, and he expressed willingness to help me.

## Where Was the Interview Conducted?

The interview was conducted via video call.

## What Did You Observe and Ask?

We asked Dee about places he regularly visits, how he discovers unfamiliar locations, and the information he uses to decide whether to visit. We also explored missing place details, trust in reviews and photos, experiences of reporting problems, feedback from organizations and authorities, and his motivations for sharing information.

He described regular motorcycle trips to a meeting point with food trucks, where he socializes with like-minded people. He mainly uses Google Search to discover places and Google Maps for locations, directions, and occasional searches for nearby services. Parking availability and distance strongly influence his choices, and he avoids some places when parking is unavailable or inconvenient. Photos help him assess the environment, seating, and cleanliness. He described an office listing that lacked an address, photos, and a map, requiring him to call the office and obtain its location through WhatsApp.

He uses the balance of positive and negative reviews, together with photos, to judge a place. He regularly posts reviews to recommend good experiences and warn others about bad ones, and he said that nothing currently discourages him from contributing. He described reporting a defaced sculpture to security and seeing it cleaned within about a day. However, he received no feedback after raising a mobile-network coverage problem with the government, which disappointed him. He also wanted more accurate traffic information. These findings reflect his self-reported experiences during the video interview.

## Needs and Possible Solutions

### 1. Check Parking Convenience Before Choosing a Place

**Possible solutions:**

- Place profiles describing car and motorcycle parking options.
- Maps showing nearby parking and walking distances to the destination.
- Recent visitor updates about parking availability and access restrictions.
- Filters for places with parking within a chosen walking distance.
- Questions to venue staff about parking arrangements before a visit.

### 2. Obtain Complete Basic Information About Places

**Possible solutions:**

- Standardized place listings containing essential details and photos.
- Simple forms allowing owners or staff to update missing information.
- Community submissions correcting incomplete or inaccurate listings.
- Questions to businesses about details that are unavailable online.
- Shareable location cards containing an address, map link, and contact information.

### 3. Assess the Environment and Quality of a Place

**Possible solutions:**

- Photo galleries identifying areas such as entrances, seating, and surroundings.
- Reviews organized around cleanliness, comfort, and service.
- Side-by-side summaries of positive and negative experiences linked to the original reviews.
- Filters for reviews describing activities or visits relevant to his plans.
- Short walkthrough videos showing the layout and environment.

### 4. Plan Journeys Using Accurate Traffic Information

**Possible solutions:**

- Current traffic conditions from available transport or traffic-data sources.
- Route comparisons showing estimated journey times and congestion.
- Comparisons of expected traffic at different departure times.
- Delay alerts for selected routes or planned journeys.
- Visible update times, data sources, and uncertainty in traffic estimates.

### 5. Receive Feedback After Reporting Public Problems

**Possible solutions:**

- Acknowledgements containing a report reference number.
- Information identifying the organization handling the report.
- Progress tracking showing whether a report is received, under review, or resolved.
- Notifications when the responsible organization replies or records an action.
- Resolution updates supported by photos or confirmation from people at the location.

### 6. Share Firsthand Experiences to Inform Other People

**Possible solutions:**

- Short review forms supporting both recommendations and warnings.
- Photo-supported reviews describing the circumstances of a visit.
- Shareable recommendations for friends and family.
- Separate options for sharing an experience and requesting action on a problem.
- Space for venues to respond to reviews and acknowledge feedback.
